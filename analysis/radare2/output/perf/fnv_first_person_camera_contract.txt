4. Player update and logical look order
---------------------------------------

PlayerCharacter's main update is 0x0093E860. The relevant established order is:

  0x0093F8D9  call 0x009445B0, player camera/input helper
  0x0093FA08  call PlayerCharacter::UpdateCamera at 0x0094AE40
  0x0093FC57  commit current movement flags through actorMover
  later       attack, activation, and other gameplay consumers continue

The direct caller pushes zero at 0x0093FA01 and again at 0x0093FA03, loads the
player receiver into ECX at 0x0093FA05, and calls at 0x0093FA08. The callee
epilogue at 0x0094C376 is `RET 8`. The exact x86 ABI is therefore
`thiscall(PlayerCharacter*, u8, u8)`. Atom's typed wrapper forwards both stack
arguments unchanged and accepts a sample only when ECX equals the live player
global; caller admission itself uses the direct-call capability in section 14.

The normal direct mouse path inside 0x009445B0 is:

  0x00945F90  call 0x00931D30, horizontal heading
  0x00945FD8  call 0x00931E50, vertical heading

Atom Input already wraps those two final heading callsites. The native path has
no general mouse smoothing stage. A first-person camera feature can sample the
coherent Atom frame, but must not re-poll DirectInput or damp the look deltas.

Because UpdateCamera completes before later attack and activation work, a
transform written into UpdateCamera's common output is not proven to be visual
only. It could become an input to logical aim, interaction, audio, or another
gameplay consumer. The common camera commit is therefore evidence, not an
approved presentation hook.


5. Normal first-person UpdateCamera branch
------------------------------------------

PlayerCharacter::UpdateCamera is 0x0094AE40.

Entry behavior:

  0x0094AE70..0x0094AE88  obtain adjusted horizontal heading through virtual
                          +0x2BC and store it at 0x011E076C
  0x0094AE94..0x0094AE99  obtain raw Actor rotX/pitch through 0x00931D70 and
                          store it at 0x011E0764

The normal matrix path independently proves those axes: 0x011E076C feeds the
0x004A0C90 Z/yaw matrix builder at 0x0094B12D, while 0x011E0764 feeds the
0x00524AC0 X/pitch matrix builder at 0x0094B13F.

The normal first-person branch is in 0x0094B12C..0x0094B38F. Reaching it
requires the ordinary state checks to select first person, including exact
zero at player +0x64C, no active +0x64A transition, and no transition byte at
0x011E07B8.

Selected observations:

  0x0094B1A9  obtain Camera1st world rotation from 0x011E07D0
  0x0094B1F1  obtain Camera1st world translation from 0x011E07D0
  0x0094B1F1..0x0094B20A
              copy Camera1st world translation into the camera-position local
  0x0094B28A..0x0094B2B2
              apply the fFirstPersonCameraMult setting at 0x011CDA28 to a
              first-person offset involving the native first-person nodes
  0x0094B38F  join the common final camera path

The Camera1st animation node is therefore a vanilla input to logical camera
construction. That does not make raw Camera1st motion a safe Atom output: it is
animation- and skeleton-dependent and it feeds the common camera result.

VATS mode 4 can construct a different camera earlier in UpdateCamera. Other
VATS modes already own selection and transition behavior. Atom must treat every
nonzero VATS mode as native camera ownership, not only playback mode 4.


6. Common logical camera commit
-------------------------------

All camera branches eventually reach a common output region. The selected
camera owner is resolved through 0x00558310 and then receives:

  0x0094BDC2  0x00440460, local translation setter
  0x0094BDD5  0x0043FA80, local rotation setter
  0x0094BE00  0x00A59C60, NiAVObject update with zeroed update data

This proves the logical camera's final local pose and update sequence. The
translation and rotation calls are shared by first person, third person, and
special branches. Hooking either call without an exact owner gate would affect
all camera modes. Even with a gate, the result remains live for downstream
player-update consumers, so it is not the selected presentation boundary.


7. Native first-person camera setup
-----------------------------------

0x00874C10 prepares the separate first-person rendering camera. It executes
immediately before RenderFirstPerson at all three direct callers.

Relevant observations:

  0x00874DF9..0x00874E05  obtain OSGlobals +0xA0
  0x00874E72              obtain Camera1st from 0x011E07D0
  0x00874E85..0x00874E98  write a world translation to OSGlobals +0xA0 camera
  0x00874EA1..0x00874EB4  write a world rotation to OSGlobals +0xA0 camera
  0x00874EB9..0x00874EBF  call 0x007721A0 to obtain player +0xE34
  0x00874EC8..0x00874EE9  reject selected animation actions and VATS
  0x00874EF6..0x00874F23  when admitted, replace first-person camera world
                          translation with the iron-sight node position
  0x00874F41..0x00874F61  temporarily replace the first-person root's local
                          translation, update it, and restore the old value

0x007721A0 is a direct accessor for PlayerCharacter +0xE34. The executable
therefore proves that native iron-sight positioning belongs to the separate
first-person rendering camera, not only to the world/logical camera.


8. World and first-person render order
--------------------------------------

The two supported main world-render callsites are:

  0x00870AE8  call RenderWorldSceneGraph at 0x00873200
  0x00870E18  call RenderWorldSceneGraph at 0x00873200

Their first-person continuation is:

  0x00870AF8  call first-person setup at 0x00874C10
  0x00870B21  call RenderFirstPerson at 0x00875110

  0x00870F33  call first-person setup at 0x00874C10
  0x00870F74  call RenderFirstPerson at 0x00875110

A third special route calls first-person setup/render without calling
0x00873200:

  0x00870927  call first-person setup at 0x00874C10
  0x0087093D  call RenderFirstPerson at 0x00875110

0x008706B0 selects between the three owner functions at 0x008707C0,
0x00870A00, and 0x00870BD0. The first route uses a different earlier scene
path. Its exact runtime purpose is not proven in this pass. A coherent Atom
world/viewmodel transaction must therefore require a token from one of the two
proven world calls; the third first-person route must remain pass-through until
classified.

RenderFirstPerson at 0x00875110:

  0x00875154  calls PlayerCharacter::Get3D(true) at 0x00950BB0
  0x008751C6  clears the first-person depth target
  0x008758EB..0x008758F1
              obtains OSGlobals +0xA0 first-person camera
  0x0087590A  calls BSShaderAccumulator::RenderFirstPersonAccumulated at
              0x00B64570 with that camera

PlayerCharacter::Get3D(true) at 0x00950BB0 returns player +0x694. False uses
the ordinary TESObjectREFR 3D path.

The world and hands/weapons are thus rendered as distinct camera-backed
stages. A wrapper at the direct RenderFirstPerson callers runs after native
Camera1st and iron-sight setup and before the first-person camera is consumed.


9. World camera source at the render boundary
---------------------------------------------

Existing OMV executable research proves:

  0x011DEB7C  holds the persistent World SceneGraph
  SceneGraph +0xAC returns the world NiCamera
  RenderWorldSceneGraph obtains that camera and passes it to renderer camera
  setup before world operations
  BSShaderManager::pCurrentCamera can change for later image-space work and is
  not the preferred persistent source at RenderWorldSceneGraph entry

The render boundary consumes the NiCamera world rotation at +0x68 and world
translation at +0x8C. A scoped wrapper can snapshot those fields, compose an
additive presentation pose, call its captured predecessor, and restore the
exact snapshot before returning. This is a derived candidate boundary. Static
evidence cannot prove its final image, culling, aim, or compatibility behavior;
those remain runtime acceptance requirements.


10. Locomotion and landing state
--------------------------------

0x009306D0 obtains an Actor's character-controller pointer through its process
virtual slot +0x28C. It returns NULL when the actor has no process.

0x005C0880 adds 0x3E0 to the bhkCharacterController and calls 0x00CA9600.
0x00CA9600 returns the 32-bit field at context +0x10. Direct engine comparisons
in the player update classify values 1 and 2 as states in which waiting is
rejected. The vendored/read-only Havok layout corroborates the enum:

  0  on ground
  1  jumping
  2  in air
  3  climbing
  4  flying
  5  swimming
  6  projectile

0x00933810 reads controller +0x3EC as the state manager and +0x3F0 as the
current state, then asks the manager for the corresponding state object. This
independently confirms the current-state storage used by 0x005C0880.

0x00812B00 is the engine's support-relative character velocity wrapper. It
receives a bhkCharacterController as ECX and a caller-owned NiPoint3 output,
calls 0x00C6E300 into an aligned Havok vector, then calls 0x00458620 to convert
that vector to the game's three-float units. It returns with RET 4.

0x00C6E300 establishes the support subtraction rather than merely returning
the controller rigid body's world velocity:

  controller +0x594       support wrapper
  support wrapper +0x08   support rigid body
  support body +0xB0      rotation basis consumed by 0x00C7F700
  controller +0x570       stored support-local velocity
  controller +0x08        character rigid body

0x00C7F700 transforms the +0x570 vector by the support basis. 0x00C6E300 locks
the character controller through 0x00C89720, obtains its rigid-body linear
velocity through 0x00CAC910, unlocks, subtracts the transformed support vector,
and writes the resulting Havok vector. 0x00812B00 performs the established
Havok-to-NiPoint3 conversion. Callers at 0x0089EF95/0x0089EFAB subtract two
actors' results, and gameplay/serialization callers at 0x0091FF93,
0x00928953, 0x0093001C, and 0x00930080 consume the same game-unit vector.

This closes the cadence carrier: it is current controller velocity relative
to the moving support, converted to ordinary game units, and copied while the
live controller is owned by the current player update. Atom retains only the
three floats. A stationary player carried by a platform therefore reports
zero horizontal gait velocity without integrating logical actor position.

Repository-owned xNVSE layout establishes TimeGlobal at 0x011F6394 and
Timer::secondsPassed at +0x0C. The first-person update samples that engine-time
delta after UpdateCamera; it does not introduce QPC or wall-clock time into
motion integration.

The process vtable established by xNVSE layout provides GetCurrentAnimAction
at +0x3E4, IsAiming at +0x404, GetKnockedState at +0x40C, and furniture state
at +0x4BC. The renderer already calls 0x008A8870 to reject weapon-animation
actions 9 and 15 through 17 during first-person setup and drawing. Atom uses
these current-owner getters only in the post-UpdateCamera sample; render hard
gates use direct process/cell/life/POV/VATS/menu/control ownership fields.

The native matrix convention is also closed by executable/OMV evidence:
NiMatrix33 occupies nine row-major floats, while camera forward, up, and right
are columns 0, 1, and 2. A camera-local vector therefore maps to world space as
`forward*x + up*y + right*z`, and a local additive rotation composes on the
right of the exact native basis. No Euler reconstruction of the native base is
required.


11. Third-party corroboration and ecosystem constraints
-------------------------------------------------------

Read-only Stewie Tweaks source implements Smooth True Iron Sights by detouring
0x00874EBF after DeferredInit. It interpolates the OSGlobals +0xA0 camera
between Camera1st and player +0xE34, gates VATS, weapon, reload, and Pip-Boy
state, and uses frame time. This corroborates the native seam and proves that a
production wrapper must preserve an existing predecessor there; Atom need not
occupy that inline site.

The same source optionally cooperates with Viewmodel Shake Fix by temporarily
zeroing the first-person player root, updating it, performing iron-sight camera
work near the origin, and restoring the root inside RenderFirstPerson through
the call at 0x00875956. This is relevant at large world coordinates. Atom must
not attach its camera directly to the animated root or duplicate that ownership
transaction.

OMV already rewrites the two world and three first-person direct CALL sites
with caller-local predecessor containers. An Atom implementation at those
sites must use the same live-target chaining rule. It must never restore a
hard-coded vanilla target over OMV or another plugin.


12. Proven consequences and remaining gates
--------------------------------------------

Proven or mechanically derived:

  - normal look input must remain direct;
  - the native logical world camera and first-person arms camera are distinct;
  - native iron-sight setup finishes before each RenderFirstPerson callsite;
  - the two main world stages finish before their paired first-person stage;
  - a render-call wrapper can be scoped more narrowly than UpdateCamera's
    common logical commit;
  - first-person mode is not established by player +0x64C alone during
    transitions;
  - VATS and the unclassified third render route require native pass-through;
  - caller-local predecessor chaining is required for OMV coexistence.

Implementation gates not closed by static research:

  - arbitrary external forced-first-person ownership beyond the explicit
    external-owner ABI and xNVSE combined disabled-control capability;
  - near-geometry clearance for any positional world-camera offset;
  - crosshair, activation, projectile, and muzzle convergence under a rendered
    camera offset;
  - culling and near-plane behavior at the maximum proposed displacement;
  - hook ordering with every installed camera/viewmodel plugin;
  - Proton/DXVK runtime behavior and subjective comfort.

At this original research stage, the support-relative velocity, engine-time,
aiming/action, matrix, and scoped transform contracts were sufficient only for
the viewmodel-first milestone. Section 16 closes a narrower Stable world-pose
decision after directly classifying the native collision helper; it does not
admit an unrestricted displaced camera or permission to infer a clearance
field or patch another mod.


13. MenuMode state contract after live admission failure
--------------------------------------------------------

Runtime observation on 2026-08-16 from replacement artifact SHA-256
6f6c2a8ac51097da8aa3bfe94b89db3f14ca9b29a5e83ee51fd7e6381dbe5fb0:

  [CAMERA] First-person viewmodel motion is unavailable:
  first-person native fingerprint mismatch at 0x00702360.

The prior player-controls warning was absent, so the replacement reader passed
admission and first-person validation reached its next ordered gate. The
on-disk FalloutNV.exe still begins 0x00702360 with `55 8B EC`; the warning
therefore proves only that the live entry differs by DeferredInit. It does not
record replacement bytes or identify an owner, and Atom must not infer one.

The radare2 MCP re-opened the identified executable at analysis level 2. Direct
disassembly and 55 call xrefs confirm a no-argument cdecl predicate returning
its boolean in AL. Its complete body is:

  0x004B7210  returns dword [0x011D8A80] (InterfaceManager singleton)
  0x009373F0  returns byte [InterfaceManager +0x00]
  0x007023A0  returns dword [InterfaceManager +0x0C] != 1

The proven native policy is therefore:

  manager != NULL && manager->active != 0 && manager->mode != 1

No camera state, allocation, ownership transfer, or side effect is hidden in
the helper. The safe intervention is no intervention at 0x00702360: validate
the InterfaceManager global slot at DeferredInit and copy those two fields in
the current main-thread camera callback. This removes an invalid byte-identity
requirement at a broadly called entry while retaining strict fingerprints for
private helpers whose exact bodies Atom actually depends on. An absent or
invalid manager returns false, matching the native helper's null policy.


14. Direct-call capability after live caller-context rejection
---------------------------------------------------------------

Runtime observation on 2026-08-16 from complete Atom artifact SHA-256
141f6947a9e6e42b92849b7f99fdca90f963f5d29f57ed3c60c7d53bb19c1959:

  15:25:16.505  [CAMERA] First-person viewmodel motion is unavailable:
                  first-person caller fingerprint mismatch at 0x00870B10.

Atom nevertheless completed DeferredInit, and the game reached normal
first-person gameplay. This accepts the artifact's startup footprint while
proving that the byte sequence immediately before the 0x00870B21 direct call
was not vanilla in the live process. It does not identify the changed bytes or
their owner.

The same run supplies an independent repository-owned capability check. At
15:25:17.509, OMV admitted the exact direct RenderFirstPerson callers
0x0087093D, 0x00870B21, and 0x00870F74 with Rel32CallHookContainer and reported
`first_person=true`. By 15:28:29 it had completed 8,476 first-person depth
captures with zero scene-transaction failures. Thus the live 0x00870B21 site
remained a callable `E8 rel32` route with the established RenderFirstPerson ABI
despite the changed surrounding bytes.

Fresh radare2 disassembly of the identified on-disk FalloutNV.exe reconfirms
the vanilla 0x00870B10 argument setup and 0x00870B21 call to 0x00875110. Static
evidence therefore describes the base executable; it is not a valid demand
that every compatible live caller retain the same argument-loading encoding.

Atom's wrappers do not consume or rewrite those caller instructions. They
forward ECX and every stack argument unchanged, apply only a render-scoped
global camera transform, call the live typed predecessor, and restore the
transform. The safe live contract is consequently the direct-call capability
already enforced by Rel32CallHookContainer: readable five-byte `E8 rel32`, an
executable decoded predecessor, unchanged captured bytes at transaction
commit, and ownership-preserving rollback. Unsupported opcodes, invalid
targets, concurrent ownership changes, and partial group installation still
fail closed. Strict byte fingerprints remain on private native helper bodies
whose implementation Atom actually consumes.


15. Runtime-output failure and bounded path proof
-------------------------------------------------

Runtime observation on 2026-08-16 from deployed artifact SHA-256
b929ce259863c27f91adcc1456b625fd47df309622de2995309a54b19b0cd85d:

  - Atom completed DeferredInit and captured vanilla predecessors for the
    UpdateCamera, both world, and all three RenderFirstPerson callsites;
  - OMV subsequently recorded more than 22,000 first-person depth stages and
    zero scene-transaction failures, proving that the shared render callers
    executed in the live process;
  - the requested Atom Input summary recorded thousands of sample-to-Present
    intervals but no camera-consumer or transformed mouse-heading samples; and
  - Atom emitted no per-frame camera evidence because that artifact recorded
    admission only.

The log therefore proves startup and render-call availability, not that Atom
accepted a native update, generated a non-identity pose, paired a route, or
wrote the first-person camera. Treating the availability line as output proof
was incorrect.

Fresh radare2 MCP inspection also corrected the unsupported name previously
assigned to player byte +0x64F. Direct executable evidence is limited to:

  0x00938A3E  initialization clears player +0x64F
  0x00940F8B  the main player update branches around ordinary work when set
  0x00942A8F  another main-update route requires it to be clear
  0x00942C0B  the camera/interaction transition route requires it to be clear
  0x0095053A  a later player helper tests it
  0x009505D5  that helper clears it after native state checks

This proves a transient native update-owner condition, not a
"force-first-person" semantic. Atom retains the fail-closed gate but reports
it with the evidence-bounded name `native special update`.

The implementation also contradicted its documented multi-render contract:
it rejected a second render solely because its immutable UpdateCamera epoch
had already been presented once. Native update rate and presentation rate are
not the same ownership domain. The route token already permits exactly one
paired RenderFirstPerson consumption and Present expires any unused token, so
the latest immutable pose is now reusable by every main world render until a
new update replaces it or ownership invalidates it.

Atom telemetry now collects fixed saturating counters for UpdateCamera calls,
accepted samples, non-identity frames, main world calls/admission, viewmodel
calls/poses, transform application, token misses, and each ordered native
sample/render rejection. Hooks perform only bounded relaxed atomics when
telemetry is enabled; they never format, allocate, lock, or log. The existing
MCM requested-summary boundary writes the copied counters and the current
combined disabled-control mask through Atom's logger. This makes the next
runtime result distinguish hook non-execution, ownership rejection, identity
generation, pairing failure, and transform failure without inspecting another
mod or adding routine render diagnostics.


16. Stable world head-motion boundary
-------------------------------------

Fresh radare2 MCP inspection on 2026-08-16 used the identified executable at
analysis level 2. The camera collision function at 0x00620BC0 has exactly one
code xref:

  0x0094A344 in PlayerCharacter camera-distance helper 0x0094A0C0

The native caller constructs the third argument with 0x00621C40, then calls:

  0x0094A32C  lea edx, [collision_result]
  0x0094A332  push edx
  0x0094A333  lea eax, [desired_endpoint]
  0x0094A336  push eax
  0x0094A337  lea ecx, [pivot]
  0x0094A33A  push ecx
  0x0094A33B  mov edx, [player]
  0x0094A33E  mov ecx, [edx + 0x21C]
  0x0094A344  call 0x00620BC0

0x00620BC0 returns with `ret 0x0C`. It converts both point arguments to Havok
space through 0x004A3E00, executes a collision query through the player-owned
world object, iterates and filters returned objects, writes the constructed
result object through its third argument, and copies the accepted hit point
back through the second point argument at 0x00620F7F..0x00620F89. It uses
0x420 bytes of aligned stack state and consults current world/player globals
during filtering. This is a mutating chase-camera transaction, not a pure
point-clearance predicate. Its sole native caller also updates global chase
distance and collision state around the query. Atom does not call it outside
that owner chain and does not invent the result object's ownership contract.

The supported first-person render seam remains the proven scoped world camera:
world calls 0x00870AE8 and 0x00870E18 consume World SceneGraph +0xAC camera
world rotation +0x68 and translation +0x8C before the paired first-person
draw. Atom snapshots those 48 bytes, composes a camera-local additive pose,
chains the live caller-local predecessor, and restores the exact snapshot.

The admitted Stable pose is intentionally narrower than a general displaced
camera:

  local forward translation  exactly zero
  local up translation       [-0.50, +0.50] game units
  local right translation    [-0.15, +0.15] game units
  roll, yaw, and pitch        exactly zero

The Stable listener therefore preserves the complete native camera basis, not
only its forward column. Native aiming or an authored first-person action
publishes exact identity world motion on that update; leaving precision state
fades motion back through the analytic aim envelope. `fCameraMotion=0` is also
exact identity.

It is a reasoned safety inference, not binary proof, that a maximum half-unit
translation stays within normal first-person near-geometry clearance. The
implementation deliberately avoids claiming a general collision-resolved
camera and requires Proton playtesting against walls, low ceilings, stairs,
doorways, multiple FOVs, and ADS before visual acceptance. Runtime telemetry
now reports generated head poses separately from world-route admission and
transform application so an admitted-but-inert artifact is directly visible.

This section established the bounded waveform but not every render-scene
owner. The later sky/hands runtime rejection and complete coordinate-space
correction in section 19 supersede its camera-only transform transaction.

artifact SHA-256

  16:37:02  First-person settings accepted camera_motion=1.00
  16:38:31  Update: calls=0, accepted=0, non_identity=0

The render wrappers and code artifact were therefore live, while the sole

replacing that one callsite; complete entry coverage handles both cases.

  0x00762F94  0x00762950             [0x011DEA3C] player global   0, 0
  0x007ED45C  0x007EC810             [0x011DEA3C] player global   0, 0
  0x007F47CA  0x007F3E00             [0x011DEA3C] player global   0, 0
  0x00879124  0x00878F60             [0x011DEA3C] player global   0, 0
  0x0093CAB7  0x0093C200             PlayerCharacter local       0, 0
  0x0093FA08  0x0093E860             PlayerCharacter local       0, 0
  0x0094014A  0x0093E860             PlayerCharacter local       0, 0
  0x00943825  0x0093E860             PlayerCharacter local       0, 0
  0x0095C80B  0x0095C730             PlayerCharacter receiver    1, 0
  0x0095F63C  0x0095F590             PlayerCharacter receiver    0, 0

The three 0x0093E860 sites are mutually reachable branches in the same large
player update. Other callers cover camera transitions, loading/cell work,
screenshot rendering, and control-state maintenance. Caller-local hooking
would require eleven independently mutable patches and would still miss a
compatible external caller. All xrefs instead converge on one function entry.

The exact entry and exit contract is:

  0x0094AE40  55                 push ebp
  0x0094AE41  8B EC              mov ebp, esp
  0x0094AE43  6A FF              push -1
  ...
  0x0094AE6A  89 8D EC FB FF FF  save ECX PlayerCharacter receiver
  0x0094AE88  D9 1D 6C071E01     store adjusted horizontal heading
  0x0094AE99  D9 1D 64071E01     store raw Actor rotX/pitch
  ...
  0x0094C366  restore SEH and nonvolatile state
  0x0094C376  C2 08 00           ret 8

All eleven callers allocate two four-byte stack argument slots, place the
PlayerCharacter receiver in ECX, and ignore EAX after the call. The function
has one converged epilogue and returns no value. The closed ABI is therefore
`thiscall(PlayerCharacter*, u8, u8) -> void`; the byte-sized semantics still
occupy ordinary x86 stack argument slots. A typed entry detour can call one
live trampoline with both arguments unchanged, wait for every native exit to
converge, and then sample the original receiver.

Atom now uses that entry as the sole sampling seam. It intentionally does not
fingerprint the mutable first five entry bytes: a compatible earlier camera
owner may already have installed a jump, and libpsycho's InlineHook relocates
the live complete instructions into a callable trampoline. A complete
first-instruction redirect at least as wide as Atom's jump is explicitly
relocatable, preserving an earlier owner's call/return chain. A short or later
terminal redirect remains unsupported because bytes after it are unreachable
and cannot establish a patch region. Atom instead validates immutable
receiver-store, logical heading/pitch-store, and sole-epilogue bytes before
preparing the hook. The same rollback transaction owns the entry hook, both
world calls, and all three first-person calls, so any preparation or ownership
failure restores every successful write and leaves the camera fully native.

Entry coverage introduces a second correctness requirement: native code may
call UpdateCamera more than once between two normal input samples. Atom Input
publishes a wrapping nonzero frame identity immediately after the native
0x0086F39E sample in the established top-level frame. The first accepted
UpdateCamera completion for that identity may advance engine time and gait
distance. Later accepted completions with the same identity are duplicates:
they invalidate any pending render-pair token but preserve the last immutable
pose and do not integrate `TimeGlobal::secondsPassed` again. A changed reset
generation or player cell takes precedence over deduplication and publishes a
fresh identity pose, preserving exact ownership reacquisition.

The entry wrapper retains no engine pointer. Sampling copies only the player
cell value, logical angles, support-relative velocity, locomotion state,
engine delta, aiming/action state, and input-frame identity. The existing
nonblocking MotionState lease remains the only mutable generator owner;
rendering reads only the atomic immutable frame. A recursive or nested camera
chain therefore either publishes the first accepted input identity or observes
it as a duplicate without creating a second mutable generator reference.

Proven by executable and runtime evidence:

  - UpdateCamera has eleven direct native callers, not one;
  - the deployed single-callsite wrapper received zero calls while the native
    UpdateCamera interior executed more than ten thousand times;
  - every native caller uses the same thiscall receiver and two stack slots;
  - all paths converge on the single `ret 8` epilogue;
  - the entry's first five bytes are whole relocatable instructions in vanilla;
  - Atom Input publishes the identity used to prevent repeated integration.

Derived design conclusion:

  - a chainable complete entry wrapper plus input-frame deduplication is the
    smallest intervention that covers every native and compatible external
    caller without eleven independently owned patches.

Still requiring runtime visual acceptance:

  - the built DLL must load through Proton and the user must confirm that the
    generated bounded pose is visible and comfortable in actual first-person
    gameplay. Static analysis, unit tests, and a Windows release build cannot
    establish pixels produced by another process.

22. Distinct render-camera and native sky-anchor identities
------------------------------------------------------------

Follow-up research date: 2026-08-17
Primary native tool: radare2 MCP, existing analysis level 2
Executable identity: unchanged from section 1

The section-20 conclusion that one World SceneGraph camera transform controls
both rendering and sky centering was incomplete. Direct disassembly proves two
different access routes:

  Render camera getter 0x006629F0
    adds 0xAC to the SceneGraph receiver and returns that pointer.

  Sky anchor getter 0x00558310
    calls 0x0045BC00 with index zero. 0x0045BC00 reads the NiTArray at
    SceneGraph +0x9C and returns child zero through 0x00877A30.

At 0x00872C55 and again at 0x00872CA6, native preparation obtains child zero
through 0x00558310, calls 0x0043C490, and copies the returned +0x58 local
translation into Sky at 0x011DEB34 and Weather at 0x011DEDA4. It then calls
NiAVObject update 0x00A59C60 on each root. 0x0043C490 itself is the direct
`this + 0x58` getter. This chain is direct executable evidence that sky
centering follows child zero, not the SceneGraph +0xAC field.

The two pointers commonly identify the same NiCamera in an unmodified scene
graph, but the executable does not require their pointer identity at either
access site. A chained camera owner may replace +0xAC while leaving child zero
resident, or may otherwise make the two transforms diverge. Atom therefore
cannot infer equality merely because vanilla normally constructs it.

The safe route transaction resolves both objects immediately before the
complete parent predecessor. It prepares finite local and world transforms for
both before the first write, mutates only one object when the pointers are
equal, and otherwise poses both. Restoration returns each exact transform to
its own object and recenters Sky and Weather from child zero's original local
translation. No pointer survives the synchronous route.

This closes the native camera-to-sky ownership contract. Whether the resulting
pixels are visually stable remains an installed Proton playtest criterion.
